How does it work?

A natural fit inside your teaching environment

No new platform to adopt, no extra handling for the teacher, no friction for the student.

  1. 01

    Native assignment

    From your institution's Moodle: Certimens is declared there as an LTI 1.3 external tool, which ships in Moodle's core, and the course takes your students straight in. Nothing to install on the platform side.

    The course names the class: the roster fills itself.

  2. 02

    Natural writing

    The student writes where they already write: Word, Google Docs, Overleaf, LibreOffice Writer, Visual Studio Code — or the editor in their Certimens space. The agent counts typing dynamics in the background.

    No text typed, no keystroke is ever recorded — and if the connection drops, the counters wait for the network to return.

  3. 03

    Objective certification

    At the deadline, the engine computes the Certimens score and delivers the certificate to the marker: the breakdown by dimension, the writing profile, and the expert report that spells it out in plain words.

    The certificate exports to PDF, and stays readable years later.

Privacy by design

The text does not exist for us

Certimens does not read, ingest or store your students' intellectual property. No text typed, no keystroke is ever recorded — only counters aggregated per measurement window, from which no content can be reconstructed. A document leaves the machine only when the student submits it. And no decision is automated: the certificate is a measuring instrument, the mark remains the teacher's.

Compatibility

Nothing to deploy on your servers

Moodle over LTI 1.3, which is in its core: the institution declares an external tool, and that is all. Sign-in can also go through your Google or Microsoft directory, or through Moodle over OAuth2. On the student side, a browser extension or an add-in is enough — and for a first assignment, not even that: the editor in the Certimens space measures right in the browser.

Sovereignty

Hosting and retention

Application data is hosted in France, at OVHcloud, and exchanges are encrypted end to end over TLS (HTTPS). Per-window measurements are destroyed one year after submission; the certificate itself remains. Every piece of data processed is listed in our privacy policy.

Use cases

Use cases for every one of your assessment formats

From the essay written over several weeks to the timed coding exam, the same measurement applies.

Take-home work, dissertations and theses

On a long piece of work, it is regularity that speaks: sessions spread over weeks, rewrites, passages reworked. The score is computed session by session, then weighted by the typing time each one carries — ten mechanical seconds buried in four hours of writing stay visible.

Word · Google Docs · Overleaf · LibreOffice

Programming exams and lab work

The Visual Studio Code extension measures the project file by file. It tells code that was written from code that was injected — which is exactly the question a development exam asks: did the student write it, or paste it?

Timed and remote exams

Under time pressure, linear transcription gives itself away: a cadence that never varies, with no hesitation and no rewriting, is the signature of a text written elsewhere and retyped here. Large arrivals of untyped text are flagged separately.

Our scientific manifesto

Faced with the urgency of AI, we chose to authenticate the human

Certimens was not born of a wish to control students, but of thinking about the science of education and cognitive psychology. Faced with the limits of text-based detectors, which get it wrong and break trust, we changed the question: no longer "does this text look like AI?", but "was this document written by the person submitting it?"

Assessment is not only a result: it is a process. We measure hesitation, restructuring and reflection — the signatures of intellectual work. Putting human cognition back at the centre of awarding degrees: that is our commitment.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does Certimens differ from classic AI detectors?

Traditional detectors settle for a statistical analysis of the final text, which produces many false positives and creates a climate of suspicion. The Certimens label draws on cognitive psychology and behavioural analysis. We certify intellectual effort by analysing the creative process itself (pause times, typing dynamics, phases of reflection), which makes for a fair and scientifically grounded assessment.

Does the analysis comply with the GDPR and respect students' privacy?

Absolutely. Regulatory compliance and data protection are at the heart of our infrastructure. Certimens records no sensitive personal data and uses no intrusive surveillance (no webcam, no browser lockdown). No text typed and no keystroke is ever recorded: only counters aggregated per measurement window, from which no content can be reconstructed. Exchanges are encrypted, and our privacy policy details every piece of data processed, its purpose and how long it is kept.

Is the tool complex to integrate with our current systems?

No. There is nothing to install on your servers: the student adds an extension to their browser for Google Docs, Word Online and Overleaf, an Office add-in for Word, the LibreOffice Writer extension, or the Visual Studio Code extension for work submitted as code — which then measures the project file by file. Each one installs from its vendor's store, and the Plugins page gathers all four, with the steps for each and manual installation for a managed fleet.

The student has no server address to type in, and no agent ever asks for their password: the agent shows a short code, the student confirms it in their Certimens space, and that is all. You can also designate your institution's online writing spaces, its Moodle or its wiki: the extension then offers them to your students, who alone can grant permission, site by site. Measurements leave in the background and are queued if the connection drops, without asking the student for anything.

And for a first assignment, or a class where nothing can be installed, there is something simpler still: the Certimens space ships an editor. The student writes in it, the document is built in Word format inside their browser, and measurement happens right there — under exactly the same rules, so that a paper written this way compares with one written in Word on equal terms. Nothing to install, nothing to authorise, and the document leaves only when the student saves it.

How is the score computed, and what does the certificate say?

The score answers one question only: was this document written by the person submitting it? It is not computed once over the totals of the assignment, but session by session, then weighted by the typing time each session carries — otherwise a few seconds of mechanical typing buried in hours of writing would be arithmetically invisible.

The certificate gives the detail: the score, its breakdown by dimension, how much of the document was actually typed, and an expert report that concludes on a writing profile — authentic, copyist (a text written elsewhere and retyped), hybrid, or undetermined where the markers form no characteristic pattern. It is an instrument of measurement: the mark itself stays the teacher's.

What does the "Label of Excellence" add to our degrees?

At a time when generative artificial intelligence makes content production trivial, a degree is only worth what it attests about the work of the person who holds it. The Certimens label documents, assessment by assessment, that the work really was written by your students: your boards deliberate on scripts whose authorship is not in question, and your institution can answer a challenge with dated facts rather than an opinion.

Can anyone check what the agent actually measures?

Yes, and deliberately so. The agents are the only part of Certimens that runs on the student's machine, and the only one whose code is public and free, under the AGPL-3.0 licence: anyone — a student, a data protection officer, your IT department — can read there what is counted, what is sent and what is not, at github.com/Certimens/plugins. A promise that cannot be checked is not a guarantee.
